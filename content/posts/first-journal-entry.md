Two oversized suitcases, a one-way ticket, and a large amount of packed coffee powder.

---

Leaving home for the first time hits in weird waves. One minute you're smugly thinking, *"Finally, a new continent, a new life, and a lot of new people to meet,"* and the next you're aggressively tearing up at the airport gate because you realize no one is going to randomly bring you filter coffee in a "tumbler-dabarah" while you study anymore. The trade-off is real, heavy, and very messy.

Walking away from the warmth, chaos, and unconditional safety net of family to essentially restart life solo in Delft is easily the most terrifying thing I've ever done. Choosing to be 6,000 miles away from the people who built me feels like a massive sacrifice. It's trading the unmatched comfort of Amma's hot meals, Sunday cricket debates and intense weekend badminton battles with Appa, and those random evenings sipping tea brewed by my sister—all for rainy bike rides, confusing Dutch grocery labels, and the silent realization that I now have to do my own adulting.

But growth rarely happens inside your comfort zone. As gut-wrenching as the goodbyes were, this leap was non-negotiable. I've spent years dreaming about this exact path, obsessing over my field, and waiting for the right moment to bet entirely on myself.

So here's to making bold, uncomfortable choices. To late-night study sessions at TU Delft, figuring out how to survive on my own, and building a future that makes everyone back home proud.

Delft, be gentle with me. 🛫🇳🇱✨
